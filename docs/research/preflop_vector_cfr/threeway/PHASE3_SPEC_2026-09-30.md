## 0. Summary

- **What.** Extend the HU step-2 trainer to 3 seats on the `3WAY50_donk*` trees, and compare its 54 charts
  with MonkerSolver's 3-way 50a set. The HU trainer is vector CFR with public chance sampling, DCFR 1.5/0/2,
  alternating updates, lazy discount and board-class rows.
- **Algorithm (section 3).**
  - Each iteration makes 3 alternating passes (UTG, CO, BTN). Each pass gets a fresh batch of 32 boards and a
    policy refresh.
  - Regret weight: w_B · P(h) · P(o1,o2|h), with P(h) = 1/465 and P(o1,o2|h) = 1/(406·351) = 1/142,506.
  - At a non-hero decision only the actor's reach vector is split. The third seat's vector passes unchanged,
    so a folded seat keeps its frozen reach: its cards are **dead**, as in step 1 and in the Monker EV probe.
  - **Hero-folded shortcut.** Once the hero has folded, its value is the constant payoff × D3 (the joint
    disjoint mass of the other two seats), and the subtree below is skipped for that hero. The shortcut is
    taken at the hero's own decision node, before any unit lookup, so a skipped unit's buffer is never read.
- **Postflop terminals.** One rank-sorted sweep per terminal, with inclusion-exclusion over the cards the two
  other hands share (section 3.4).
  - Cost is O(n·k) with k ≤ 36 card terms per hand, not O(n³).
  - In short deck about 99 % of live hands share their rank with another hand, so the tie terms run on almost
    every hand. About **375-520 Mflop per board and hero pass** (*estimate*), against about 1 Mflop in HU.
- **Preflop terminals** (36 all-ins, 25 folds, and the preflop shortcut values) come from a **per-pass class
  cache** built on `preflop_three_way_v1.bin`.
  - It is exact over all runouts, with dead folded cards.
  - Values are scaled by C(30,5)/C(34,5) = 0.512140 so that they stay unbiased under the board-conditioned
    weight.
  - A validation-only mode (`preflop_terminals = board_kernels`) evaluates the same terminals on the listed
    board with the multiway kernels. It is what makes the step-1 comparison (V9) test the kernels.
- **Rake** is already in the engine's payoff rows. At creation the trainer checks that a losing seat has a
  single payoff.
- **Memory (section 4).**
  - 3WAY50 at 15×4: 847,242,189 cells, 13.56 GB in double. Peak *about 14.8 GB*: fits the i3.
  - 30×4: 27.11 GB in double, peak *about 29.2 GB*: server only in double. On the i3 it needs mixed (*22.4 GB*)
    or float32 (*15.6 GB*) storage.
  - If the Monker-like texture map TXM (1,899 turn classes) is adopted, 30×4 drops to 12.0 GB in double.
- **Iterations (section 5).**
  - Updates per row do not depend on tree size: per hero pass, every node's rows get the same 32 boards as in
    HU. So 3-way needs about the HU iteration count *at the same convergence level*.
  - The HU runs stopped on the **all-chart** mean (0.005); at those stops their **non-all-in** charts were
    still changing by 0.0073-0.0085 (critique 2). The HU-equivalent non-all-in threshold is therefore
    **0.008**, and with it the expected stop stays at 24,000-40,000 (*estimate*). Keeping 0.005 on the
    non-all-in charts means 40,000-56,000 iterations.
  - Time per iteration (*estimate*, planning): **2-4.5 s on the i3** and 0.4-1.0 s on the server. A
    24,000-40,000-iteration run takes 13-50 h on the i3 (**one to three daily windows**, most likely two) and
    2.7-11 h on the server.
- **Stop rule (D3).** Recommended: stop when the mean chart change of the **18 non-all-in charts** is below
  **0.008** between snapshots 4,000 iterations apart (HU-equivalent). Log the 54-, 36- and 18-chart means at
  every snapshot. Minimum 16,000 iterations, cap 48,000. Option: 0.005 on the 18 charts, cap 64,000.
- **Evaluation (section 6).**
  - Every snapshot: distance, range difference, suited preference at both first-in roots, first-in mixes.
  - Part A reuses the trainer's fixed-policy traversal over a board list: exact over 605,088 boards, or sampled
    on **physical** flops. It gives:
    - per-seat EV and the rake identity;
    - each seat's preflop-only best-response gain;
    - MonkerSolver's charts played in our game;
    - the 3-seat lock-test metric.
  - It needs its own initialization path (policy only, no regrets): peak about 7.3 GB at 15×4 and 14.1 GB at
    30×4, against 14.1 / 27.7 GB on the current fixed-policy path.
  - Cost (*estimate*): exact 1-2.5 h on the server or 8-18 h on the i3; sampled on 64 physical flops 0.9-2 h
    on the i3. On the i3 it never shares a window with training.
  - Part B (full per-seat best response and NashConv) comes later, on the server only.
- **Validation (section 7).** Thirteen checks plus V9b, including:
  - HU byte identity;
  - brute force of every kernel, with tolerances scaled by the deal mass;
  - "HU plus a uniform dead third hand = HU";
  - equality with the step-1 class solver on the checkdown tree **with the preflop terminals evaluated by the

## 3. Algorithm

### 3.1 Vector CFR with public chance sampling, 3 seats

**Iteration.**
1. DCFR discount once, lazily, as now (`trainer.cpp:1322-1336`).
2. For hero = 0, 1, 2 in that fixed order:
   1. Draw a fresh batch of 32 boards. In HU only the second hero redraws (`:1395-1407`); here every hero after
      the first redraws.
   2. Prepare the boards.
   3. Refresh the compact policy.
   4. Rebuild the class cache (section 3.9).
   5. Make one pass per board.

This keeps the HU argument: each hero's counterfactual values are unbiased conditional on the policies that the
earlier heroes have just updated. That makes 3 batches, 3 refreshes and 96 board passes per iteration (HU: 2,
2 and 64).

- **Simultaneous updates** (one batch and one snapshot for all three) would save 2 refreshes and 64 board
  preparations. It is kept only as an option, not recommended: every HU result, and the step-1 class solver,
  use alternating updates.
- **Seat order** is part of the trainer identity.

**Weights (the contract of `trainer.hpp:20-60`, generalized).** For board B of weight w_B, hero hand h, and
the other seats' hands (o1, o2), mutually disjoint and disjoint from B:

```
R[n][row(h)][a] += w_B · P(h) · P(o1,o2|h) · (v_a[h] − v[h])      P(h) = 1/465, P(o1,o2|h) = 1/(406·351)
S[n][row(h)][a] += w_B · P(h) · reach_hero[h] · σ(a | n, row(h))
v[h] = Σ over ordered pairs (o1 of the lower other seat, o2 of the higher) of
       r_lower(o1) · r_higher(o2) · u_hero(h, o1, o2, B)
```

- The values sum over the 142,506 ordered disjoint pairs, and the weight brings the increments back to antes.
- Hand subsets and diagnostic board priors (`trainer.cpp:72-111`, `:636-654`) are **rejected** for N = 3,
  because they would need disjoint-pair counts per hand.
- **Weighted board lists** (`TrainingBoards` with `sample = false`) are **allowed** with full ranges. Their
  weights are per board, and part A, V9 and V9b need them. The exact path makes the whole list one batch
  (`trainer.cpp:1378-1381`); for N = 3 every hero reuses it, as the HU exact path does. Weights are normalized
  to sum 1 (orbit size / 376,992 for the 19,998 canonical 5-card boards).

**Reach.** Each unit, and each traversal level, carries three reach vectors, indexed by seat:
- `Unit` gets `reach[3][465]`: 11 KB per unit.
- At a decision of seat s ≠ hero: split `reach[s]` by s's policy rows, pass the third vector unchanged, and
  sum the child values. This is valid because terminal values are multilinear in the other seats' reach.
- At a hero decision: split `reach[hero]` as now.
- **A folded seat never acts again, so its vector is frozen from the moment it folds.** That is exactly the
  "dead cards" convention, jointly with the board.

**Pruning and gates** (the HU logic at `:1570-1576`, `:1588-1590` and `:1693`, generalized):
- `others_zero` = either other seat's reach is all zero. Every value is then zero, since values are products.
- Prune when `others_zero` holds and the hero's reach is all zero.
- Skip terminals when `others_zero` holds.
- Write regrets only when `others_zero` does not hold.
- Strategy sums keep the `reach_hero > 0` gate (`:1706`).

**HU path unchanged.** `iterate`, `pass`, `top_down_reach`, `traverse` and `terminal` stay byte-for-byte as
they are for N = 2. The 3-seat path is a separate set of member functions (`pass3`, `top_down_reach3`,
`traverse3`, `terminal3`), dispatched on `player_count`. A per-board duplicate is cheaper than proving that a
generic rewrite reproduces the HU bits (section 11, item 10).

### 3.2 Hero-folded shortcut

When the hero folds at a node where two other seats stay active, every terminal below pays the hero the same
amount, −(hero's commitment). The shared-pot rule gives this, and step 1 checks it at `checkdown_classes.hpp`
(the loser-payoff checks inside `three_way_terminals`).

The hero's value at that child is therefore:

```
v[h] = payoff_hero(child) · D3(h),    D3(h) = Σ_{o1,o2 mutually disjoint, disjoint from h} r_lower(o1)·r_higher(o2)
```

The reach vectors are those at the child. The sum over the subtree's terminals equals D3 at the child because
splitting a reach vector by a policy row preserves its sum, and the board is fixed within a pass. There is no
hero decision below, so the subtree is skipped.

- **At creation**, for every node where some seat is inactive, record `folded_payoff[node][seat]` and assert
  that every terminal below gives the same value.
- **Where the shortcut is taken (ordering rule).** In `traverse3`, at a hero decision node, for every action
  whose child is not a terminal and leaves the hero inactive, set v_a = `folded_payoff[child][hero]` × D3 (D3
  cached per level at this node, section 3.7) and **do not recurse** into the child. `traverse3` therefore
  never enters a hero-inactive node, in the top phase or in a unit; its entry asserts (debug builds) that the
  hero bit is in `node.active_mask`.
  - This matters because the HU `traverse` returns a unit's stored values **before** it looks at the node
    (`trainer.cpp:1562-1567`), and `SubtreePartition::build` (`:428-457`) makes every child of a top node with
    at most `target` nodes a unit root. A hero's postflop fold child is often such a root. If the shortcut
    were applied after the unit lookup, the skipped unit's buffer (values of the previous board or hero)
    would flow into the regrets without any error.
  - Every hero-inactive subtree starts at an edge where the hero folds, so the rule covers all of them,
    including the preflop ones.
- **Units:** `top_down_reach3` does not descend into hero-inactive subtrees; each unit whose root lies in one
  is marked in `unit_skipped[hero]` and neither prepared nor run in that hero's parallel phase. Tests fill the
  buffers of skipped units with NaN (V6, V10), so any read becomes visible.
- **Effect:** 11 of the 15 flop entries are 2-way pots (23.4 % of the cells). Each one, and every postflop
  subtree after a hero fold in a 3-way pot, is walked by only 2 of the 3 heroes.
- **Check:** V6 compares values with the shortcut on and off, at the production partition target and with
  several threads.

### 3.4 The pair primitive M(S1, S2)

For a hero hand h = {x, y} on board B, two seat reach vectors r1 and r2 over the n live hands, and hand sets S1
and S2 (defined by rank relative to h), define:

```
M(S1,S2)[h] = Σ_{o1 ∈ S1, o2 ∈ S2; o1, o2, h mutually disjoint} r1(o1) · r2(o2)
```

By inclusion-exclusion over the cards that o1 and o2 share (both already disjoint from h), this is the
combo-level twin of `three_way_deal_values`, `checkdown_classes.hpp:181-242`:

```
M(S1,S2)[h] = A_h · B_h  −  Σ_{c ∉ h} A_h(c) · B_h(c)  +  P_h
  A_h    = Σ_{o ∈ S1, o ∩ h = ∅} r1(o)       = S1tot − C1[x] − C1[y] + [h ∈ S1]·r1(h)
  A_h(c) = Σ_{o ∈ S1, c ∈ o, o ∩ h = ∅} r1(o) = C1[c] − Pm1[c][x] − Pm1[c][y]            (c ∉ h)
  P_h    = Σ_{o ∈ S1∩S2, o ∩ h = ∅} r1(o)·r2(o) = Ptot − Pc[x] − Pc[y] + [h ∈ S1∩S2]·r1(h)·r2(h)
```

B_h and B_h(c) are the same with S2 and r2.
- C[c] is the reach mass of the set's hands holding card c.
- Pm[c][d] = r(hand {c,d}) if that hand is in the set, else 0. It is a 36×36 matrix with 2 entries per hand.
- Pc and Ptot are the same sums over the product r1·r2.
- **Why it works:** a pair (o1, o2) sharing exactly one card c is removed once by the card sum. The pair
  o1 = o2 is removed twice (once per card), and P_h adds it back once.
- **Branch-free form:** sum over all 36 cards (board cards have zero entries). Then subtract the two terms
  c ∈ {x, y} explicitly, each O(1): the c = x term is (C1[x] − Pm1[x][y])·(C2[x] − Pm2[x][y]), since
  Pm[x][x] = 0.
- **Cost per hand and per M:** 36 terms of about 5 flops, about 150-180 flops. With the matrices stored
  row-major and padded to 36 doubles, row x of Pm is contiguous: 9 AVX2 vectors per operand.

**Static sets** (All): D3 and the folded seat's side of section 3.6 can use a precomputed G = Pm1ᵀ·Pm2.
- The sparse product costs Σ_c 30 × 30 ≈ 28,000 MACs per terminal.
- The cross term then costs O(1) per hand: S0 − P1[x] − P1[y] − P2[x] − P2[y] + G[x][x] + G[x][y] +
  G[y][x] + G[y][y], minus the two c ∈ h terms.
- The totals are about 35,000 flops against about 70,000 for the direct loop.
- Optional: the microbenchmark decides.

**Dynamic sets** (Below and Group, during the sweep): the direct 36-term loop.
- Inserting a hand into a set is O(1): C[p], C[q], Pm[p][q], Pm[q][p], Pc and the totals.
- Maintaining G incrementally would cost about 240 flops per insertion, more than the direct query.

### 3.5 3-active showdown: one ascending sweep

For a hero whose other seats are lower (ℓ) and higher (u), with reach r_ℓ and r_u:
- **Order:** the board's rank order is built once per board (`board_context.cpp:63-77`). Add rank-group start
  positions (`BoardContext` counts the groups at `:70-76` but does not store them) and a 36×36
  card-pair → live-hand index.
- **Sets:** B = strictly weaker hands (Below), G = hands of the same rank (Group, h included), All = every live
  hand.

```
precompute D[h] = M(All,All)[h] with (r_ℓ, r_u)                          (static; direct or G form)
Below := ∅ for both seats (totals, per-card sums C, pair matrices Pm, product sums Pc)
for each rank group g in ascending order:
    build Group structures from the members of g (both seats)             (O(|g|))
    for each h in g:
        W[h]   = M(B,B)[h]                                                 hero beats both
        if |g| > 1:
            Tℓ[h]  = M(G,B)[h]    ties ℓ, beats u      (P term 0: G ∩ B = ∅)
            Tu[h]  = M(B,G)[h]    beats ℓ, ties u
            Tℓu[h] = M(G,G)[h]    ties both            (P term with [h ∈ G] = 1)
        else Tℓ = Tu = Tℓu = 0
        L[h]   = D[h] − W − Tℓ − Tu − Tℓu                                  hero loses to at least one
    insert the members of g into Below; clear Group
v[h] = W·p({h}) + Tℓ·p({h,ℓ}) + Tu·p({h,u}) + Tℓu·p({h,ℓ,u}) + L·p_lose
```

- p_lose is checked equal over {ℓ}, {u} and {ℓ,u} at creation (one pot, equal stacks), as step 1 does.
- **Ties are the common case in short deck.** Rechecked here with an inline Python evaluator (40 random
  boards, seed 1): only **1.4 %** of the 465 live hands are alone in their rank group; there are 35.6 groups
  per board (3-60), the hand-weighted mean group size is 47 and the largest group has 351 hands. The
  `|g| > 1` test stays (it is free), but it saves about 1 %, not 60-80 % as the first version assumed.
- **Fused loop (required layout).** For each h, one pass over the 36 cards computes the four per-card terms
  A_B(c), A_G(c) (lower seat, Below and Group) and B_B(c), B_G(c) (higher seat), each two subtractions, and
  accumulates the four products W, Tℓ, Tu, Tℓu with four FMAs: about 16 flops per card, 576 per hand, plus the
  O(1) totals, the c ∈ h corrections and the P terms. The same loop order serves the AVX2 layout of 3.4.
- **Rounding.** L and (in 3.6) Lose come out of a subtraction. When their exact value is 0 or small, the
  computed value can be −O(1e-11)·D[h] (critique 1 replica: −1.5e-11 to +4.5e-12 on D ≈ 2.5e4). They are not
  clamped (clamping would bias), and tests use the D-scaled tolerance of section 7.
- **Cost:** fused W + three ties about 576-600 flops, plus D about 75-150 (static): **about 650-730 flops per
  hand fused, 675-870 unfused, 0.30-0.40 Mflop per terminal per hero** (*estimate*). The HU sweep is about 6
  flops per hand.

### 3.6 2-active showdown after a fold, folded hand dead

The hero is active, o is the active opponent, and f has folded; f's reach is frozen.

```
D3[h]  = M(All_o, All_f)[h]                         (static)
Win[h] = M(Below_o, All_f)[h]                       (dynamic on o's side, static on f's side)
Tie[h] = M(Group_o, All_f)[h]      if |g| > 1, else 0
Lose[h] = D3 − Win − Tie
v[h] = Win·p({h}) + Tie·p({h,o}) + Lose·p({o})
```

- This is the same ascending sweep with S2 = All_f. f's per-card sums and pair matrix are static per terminal.
- It removes f's cards jointly with the board and with h and o, which is the convention the verifier confirmed
  on MonkerSolver's 60a EVs (joint removal, diary 5.9).
- It covers the 2-active terminals in 3-way pots and all terminals of the 11 two-way flop entries. There, f is
  the preflop folder and its reach is its class-constant preflop fold reach.
- **Fused loop.** f's per-card terms F(c) are static per terminal; one pass over the 36 cards computes
  A_B(c) and A_G(c) for o and accumulates Win and Tie against F(c) (about 10 flops per card). D3 uses the G
  form of 3.4.
- **Cost:** Tie runs on about 99 % of hands (3.5), so about **375-510 flops per hand, 0.17-0.24 Mflop per
  terminal** (*estimate*; about 400 fused).
- **Identity** (V3, and the basis of the V7 harness): with r_f ≡ 1 on every live hand, Win = 351 × HU worse
  mass, Tie = 351 × tied mass, and D3 = 351 × `fold_mass`. There are C(27,2) = 351 hands disjoint from the
  board, h and o.

### 3.7 Folds

- **Hero is the last seat standing:** fold payoff × D3 with the reach at the terminal.
- **Hero is the seat that folds into the terminal:** fold payoff × D3 of the parent node. D3 depends only on
  the other seats' reach, which the hero's action does not change, so it is cached per level at the hero's
  decision node and reused by every fold child.
- **Cost:** D3 alone, about 75-150 flops per hand.

### 3.8 Postflop all-in runouts

- A flop or turn all-in is a `TerminalShowdown` with cards still to come (`compiled_game.cpp:112-114`). As in
  HU, it is evaluated on the sampled full 5-card board, which is its runout, using the kernels of 3.5 and 3.6.
- This is unbiased because the board sampling is the chance model. It is not Rao-Blackwellized over runouts,
  which HU does not do either.

### 3.9 Preflop terminals: per-pass class cache

**Why it is exact.**
- Preflop rows are the 81 classes: `traverse` uses `hand_classes()[hand]` as the preflop slot
  (`trainer.cpp:1639-1640`), and initial reach is 1. So every seat's reach at every preflop node is constant
  over the combos of a class.
- The step-1 argument (phase 2a spec §2.1) then applies inside each pass: each hero's value at a preflop
  terminal depends only on its class and on the class reach of the other seats.
- The 3-player class table gives the exact expectation over all 142,506 runouts, with folded cards dead.

**Build, once at trainer creation.**
- Port the tensor builder of `three_way_terminals` (`checkdown_classes.hpp:476-600`, `FoldedCards::Dead`) into
  the library, as a new `preflop_class_cache.{hpp,cpp}` in `libs/preflop_blueprint`. `card_abstraction` is an
  allowed dependency (`libs/preflop_blueprint/CMakeLists.txt:37-40`).
- It **cannot be moved unchanged**: on the step-2 tree it throws at the first postflop showdown ("a 3-way
  checkdown showdown has five cards to come", `checkdown_classes.hpp:535-536`), and its fold branch
  (`:517-524`) gives a class term to every `TerminalFold`, including the 1,375 postflop folds. The library
  version builds terms **only for nodes with `street == Preflop`** and asserts the counts: on 3WAY50, exactly
  36 all-in showdown terms and 25 fold terms; on the checkdown-compiled 3WAY50, the same plus the 15 checkdown
  leaves (which are preflop showdowns with 5 cards to come, `compiled_game.cpp:124-128`).
- One double tensor [H][A][B] per (preflop showdown terminal, active seat): 9 × 3 + 27 × 2 = **81 tensors of
  4.25 MB = 0.344 GB**.
- Folds and folded seats use the class D3 contraction (`three_way_deal_values`, about 5,000 operations), not
  a tensor.
- The checks of step 1 carry over: loser payoff, folder payoff, and the transpose when the folder is the lower
  seat.

**Per hero pass** (after `refresh_policy`, which has already applied any lock):
1. Compute the class reach of each seat at every preflop node from the compact policy's preflop rows: 130
   nodes × 3 × 81.
2. Contract, in a fixed order:
   - for each preflop all-in terminal where the hero is active, `values[H] = Σ_{A,B} ρℓ[A]·ρu[B]·K[H][A][B]`
     (the interleaved contraction of step 1);
   - for folds and hero-folded preflop children, payoff × class D3.
3. Cost: at most about 27 tensors × 531,441 MACs, about 14 M MACs, **5-10 ms per pass and 15-30 ms per
   iteration** (*estimate*).

**Scale.** The trainer weights a deal by 1/(406·351) = 1/142,506, and sums only over opponents that are live on
the sampled board. The class values sum over every disjoint pair: 561 × 496 = 278,256 pairs.

For a fixed h, P(o1 and o2 both live | h live) = C(30,5)/C(34,5) = 142,506/278,256. So

```
cache_value[h] = 0.512140 × Σ_{all disjoint (o1,o2)} r(o1)·r(o2)·E_runouts[u(h,o1,o2)]
```

has the same expectation over boards as the board-restricted sum. This is the tower property that HU already
relies on, with its factor 406/561.

**Choice.** All preflop terminal values come from the cache, so they are deterministic given the policy:
- the 36 all-in-facing charts get no board noise at all;
- the fold/call regret difference in those nodes cancels exactly.

HU instead restricts opponents to the board, including at preflop folds. Mixing a board-free all-in value with
a board-restricted fold value would leave blocker noise in exactly the decisions that should converge fastest.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- Evaluate preflop all-ins on the sampled board with the 3.5/3.6 kernels (MAP 2's proposal) in production. It
  is unbiased, but one runout per board adds 0/1 variance to decisions that are exact today. It is kept as the
  validation mode below.
- A combo-level triple table: 29.2 M unordered triples, about 1.05 GB. It is not needed.

**Validation-only mode: `preflop_terminals = board_kernels`.** Without it the cache makes the step-1
comparison circular: on the checkdown tree every leaf is a `TerminalShowdown` with `street == Preflop`
(`compiled_game.cpp:124-128`), every such terminal takes the preflop path (`trainer.cpp:1804` in HU; the
cache in 3.9), and the cache is built by the same code as the class solver's tensors. The 3-seat trainer would
then call none of the kernels of 3.5-3.7 on that tree, and since all its values would be board-free, one board
would give the same trajectory as 19,998 (regret matching and DCFR are scale-invariant per row).
- `TrainerConfig::preflop_terminals` ∈ {`class_cache` (default), `board_kernels`}. In `board_kernels`, every
  `street == Preflop` showdown (all-ins and checkdown leaves) is evaluated on the listed board, which is its
  runout, with the kernels of 3.5 (3 active) and 3.6 (2 active, folder dead); preflop folds and hero-folded
  preflop children use the board-restricted D3 (M(All, All) on the board) instead of the class D3.
- It is part of the trainer identity (`preflop-terminals=board-kernels`) and requires
  `TrainerConfig::validation = true`, also in the identity; the train CLI accepts it only with `--validation`,
  the runner never passes it, and a production configuration cannot resume such a checkpoint (identity
  mismatch).
- Over the exact list of the 19,998 suit-canonical 5-card boards with orbit weights (total 376,992), it is
  exact for class rows: class-constant reach is suit-symmetric, so a class row's increment is the same on
  every board of an orbit.

### 3.10 Rake per terminal

- Rake is inside `showdown_payoffs` and `fold_payoffs`: `settle_terminal`, `flop_dealt = street != Preflop ||
  AllInRunout` (diary 5.9). The trainer adds nothing.
- New checks at creation, for N = 3:
  - the loser payoff is constant over the winner sets that exclude the seat;
  - the folded payoff is constant below a fold (3.2).
- The per-terminal identity (the seats' payoffs sum to minus the rake) is already tested on the tree
  (`tests/preflop_blueprint_game_tests.cpp:1813-1860`, 3,701 postflop terminals).
- V8 adds the vector identity at the kernel level, and part A adds the expected-rake identity.
- Do **not** reuse `check_terminal_rake` or `expected_rake_by_reach` from `checkdown_classes.hpp`
  (`:339-374`, `:976-1002`): they pass `kind == TerminalShowdown` as `flop_dealt`, which is wrong for
  postflop folds (MAP 3).

### 3.12 Parallelism and determinism

- **Unchanged:** parallel units over disjoint subtrees within a board, boards in order, one writer per cell.
  The result is bit-identical for any thread count (`trainer.hpp:31-34`).
- **Kernels:** each terminal is computed by one thread in a fixed order.
- **Class cache:** each tensor is contracted in one piece.
- **Partition:** 3-active subtrees have terminals about 30-80 times costlier than HU nodes (*estimate*).
  - Weigh units by estimated cost (terminal kind × active count) instead of node count, or lower
    `--partition-target` (the runner passes 64).
  - Optional; measure the imbalance in the smoke run first (`detailed_profile` timings per unit).
- **Skipped units** (3.2) are excluded from the work list of the hero's parallel phase; their buffers are not
  written. Determinism is unaffected because the skip set depends only on the tree and the hero.
- **Scratch per thread:** 5 × 465 doubles plus six 36×36 matrices, about 60 KB.
- **Exact board lists:** the whole list is one batch (`trainer.cpp:1378-1381`); a `BoardWork` holds the
  context, 3 × 3 per-hand arrays and the compact slots, *about* 40-60 KB for N = 3, so the 19,998-board list of
  V9 takes *about* 1 GB. Part A processes its 605,088 boards per flop chunk (1,056 boards, *about* 60 MB).

## 5. Iteration budget, time and stop rule

### 5.1 Updates per information set (I/N study, lane B)

**What one iteration updates** (code read and replicated exactly, `research_results.md` RESULT 1 §2-3,
RESULT 3 §2):
- Each hero pass updates, at **every** hero decision node, the rows of each of the 32 sampled boards' class.
- So the number of board samples per row does not depend on how many nodes the tree has.
- Per row and per iteration, measured in HU (15×4, per hero pass of 32 boards):
  - flop: 0.054·T board samples (g1_rake: mean 2,060 at T = 37,850);
  - turn: 0.0066·T;
  - river: 0.0066·T at 15 river levels, 0.0057·T at 30 levels (m30x4_rake25 at T = 32,117).

**3-way.** Each of the 3 heroes gets its own 32 boards per iteration. The per-row counts at every 3-way node
are therefore the **same functions of T as in HU**, for the same abstraction:
- flop about 0.054·T;
- turn about 0.006·T;
- river about 0.006·T.

The tree is 13.9 times HU in rows, but the rows are spread over more nodes, not thinned per node.

- **Monker-style global I/N** (the hand-level scale, 32·T·465/N) is 13.9 times lower than HU's for the same
  T: 1.24 for 3WAY50 at 15×4 against 17.3 for HU at T = 32,000. MonkerSolver's guideline of 10 I/N counts
  iterations of a single-deal sampler, so it does not transfer. The per-row counts are the relevant measure
  for this trainer. Report both.
- **Reach.** Multiway lines have lower opponent reach. With vector CFR, reach scales signal and noise of a
  row's regret increments together, so it does not change the number of samples a row needs (*inference*).
  The zero-reach skips (`trainer.cpp:1693`) are not logged, so the per-row counts are upper bounds.
- **30×4 against 15×4.** Flop per-row counts barely depend on the buckets. The number of hands per row-board
  update drops (flop 12.2 → 7.3), and on the hand-level scale 30×4 needs about **2×** the iterations (lane B).
  In practice the HU 30×4 run with rake 2.5 % / cap 2a stopped at 32,000, the same point as G1 with rake at
  15×4.

**What the HU stops mean for the non-all-in charts** (critique 2, per-chart changes recomputed from
`out/monker/variants/*/charts/it_*/vs_previous.json`, 4 non-all-in and 4 all-in HU charts):
- HU50_m30x4_rake25 at 32,000: all charts 0.0045 (its stop), non-all-in 0.0073, all-in 0.0017. The mean of the
  two is 0.0045, so the figures are consistent.
- HU50_g1_rake at 32,000: all charts 0.0049 (its stop), non-all-in 0.0085; at 36,000 non-all-in 0.0054.
- HU50_g1plus at 28,000: non-all-in 0.0076.
- m30x4's non-all-in change decays by about 0.70 per 4,000 iterations (0.0104 → 0.0073).

So the HU stops, made with the all-chart mean at 0.005, correspond to a **non-all-in change of 0.0073-0.0085**.
No HU run would have stopped where it did under "0.005 on the non-all-in charts"; that rule's HU-equivalent
stop is 36,000-44,000, before any 3-player effect.

**Budget** (*estimate*), by stop rule (D3):
- **Recommended, 0.008 on the 18 non-all-in charts** (HU-equivalent; it would have stopped the HU runs at
  28,000-36,000):
  - 15×4: stop expected at **24,000-40,000**;
  - 30×4: 32,000-48,000;
  - cap 48,000.
- **Alternative, 0.005 on the 18 non-all-in charts:**
  - 15×4: 40,000-56,000; 30×4: 44,000-64,000;

### 5.3 Stop rule

The rule (diary 5.7 proposed a 3-way rule; still open, 8, item 5), revised after critique 2:
- **Stop** when the mean chart change between consecutive snapshots 4,000 iterations apart is **below 0.008**,
  **computed over the 18 non-all-in charts** (recommended; D3 offers 0.005 with cap 64,000).
- Log the 54-chart, 36-all-in and 18-non-all-in means at **every** snapshot, so the run can be compared with
  the HU runs on any of the three.
- **Minimum** 16,000 iterations (4 snapshots), **cap** 48,000.
- A run stopped by the 0.008 rule can be resumed from its checkpoint toward 0.005 later (for example on the
  server) without changing anything else.

Why the 18 charts, and why 0.008:
- 36 of the 54 files face an all-in: their action set is only fold and call (manifest count at 13:24). They
  depend only on equity, rake and ranges, and with the class cache they converge like step 1.
- In 3-way the all-in charts are two thirds of the 54, against half of the 8 in HU, so a 54-chart mean of
  0.005 would be much looser than the HU rule: it could hide changes of about 0.012-0.015 in the 18 charts
  that depend on the postflop (MAP 3).
- The HU stops correspond to a non-all-in change of 0.0073-0.0085 (5.1). 0.008 on the 18 charts is therefore
  the HU-equivalent threshold; 0.005 on the 18 charts is stricter than anything the HU results were obtained
  with.
- The chart-change mean is measured at a 4,000-iteration spacing in HU. Keep that spacing so the threshold
  keeps its meaning.

Implementation:
- `compare_charts.py` gets `non_all_in_mean_distance` and `all_in_mean_distance`. A chart is "facing all-in"
  when its columns are exactly {Call, Fold}.
- The runner gets `STOP_METRIC=non_all_in`, and is edited only while no run uses it. Long runs use the frozen
  copy in `out/frozen/`.

Lock runs: every locked chart has change 0, so use `THRESHOLD=0` and a fixed `MAX` **per abstraction**:
- 24,000 at 15×4 (HU test 5 plateaued: 1.44 / 1.43 / 1.45 % of the pot at 24,000 / 40,000 / 64,000, at 15×4);
- **48,000 at 30×4**: HU test 7 (30×4, rake 2.5 % / cap 2a) hit `ITERATION_LIMIT` at 24,000 (13:28:19) and was
  resumed to `max 48000` at 13:38:14 (critique 2, `run.log`); lane B puts its hand-level I/N at 24,000 at 6.5
  against 12.9 for the 15×4 lock, i.e. 30×4 needs about 2× the iterations.
- Revise both once test 7's gains at 24,000 and 48,000 are compared.

### 5.4 Daily window on the i3

Runs are allowed only from 00:00 to 20:00, so a 3-way run spans days:
- a queued STOP file at **19:40** makes the runner checkpoint and exit (checkpoint only, 1.3-4.5 min at 15×4);
  the first version's 19:55 did not leave room for a 2-7 min save at the measured rates;
- a frozen queue script resumes from the checkpoint at 00:00, with a cancel file (memory rule
  `taskstop-orphans`: queued scripts are stopped by PID and checked);
- no archive to F: and no build while a save is running; archives run 20:00-24:00.

## 6. Evaluation

### 6.1 What is exact and what is sampled

| Measure | 3 seats | Cost | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charts: distance, range difference, suited preference, first-in mixes, tree check | exact (chart files) | seconds | every snapshot, both machines |
| Part A: per-seat EV, rake identity, per-seat preflop action values → preflop-only best response per seat, MonkerSolver in our game, lock-test metric | exact over 605,088 boards (573 canonical flops × 33 × 32, orbit weights) | *8-18 h i3; 1-2.5 h server* | final policy; server every 1-2 snapshots |
| Part A, sampled | K **physical** flops drawn uniformly from the 7,140 with a fixed seed (paired across snapshots) × all 1,056 turn-river runouts, weight 1/K each | 64 flops (67,584 boards, 11 %): *0.9-2 h i3, 6-17 min server* | i3 curves |
| Part B: full per-seat best response, NashConv, `--exploit` | exact (street-aggregated evaluator) | *2-5 h per pass, server* | phase 3c |

- Part A costs were rescaled with the kernel correction of 3.11: 605,088 boards × 3 seats × 375-520 Mflop =
  680-945 Tflop, 3.8-13 h at 20-50 GFLOP/s, plus 3-4.5 h of traversal (*estimate*).
- **Sampling must be by physical flop** (as the HU evaluator: `sample_physical_history`,
  `preflop_blueprint_monker_values.cpp:774`, and its header, `:13-16`). The flop is canonicalized only to share
  cached work; each sampled flop has weight 1/K. A uniform draw over the 573 canonical flops reweighted by
  orbit size normalized over the sample would be a biased ratio estimator, and an unweighted one would
  over-represent monotone and paired flops. Equivalent alternative: draw canonical flops with probability
  orbit/7,140 and weight 1/K.
- With that sampling, linear measures (EV, action values, chart loss) are unbiased. Standard errors come from
  the per-flop contributions (flops are i.i.d.).
- Best-response gains are a maximum over noisy values, so they keep an upward bias. Report them as upper
  estimates.
- With 3 players CFR has no Nash guarantee (as in step 1). The per-seat gain is the quality measure; the
  NashConv sum is reported without meaning "distance to equilibrium" in the 2-player sense.

### 6.2 Part A design: the average-profile pass through the trainer

**Mode.** A new `Trainer::evaluate_policy_values(policy, board list, sink)` in fixed-policy mode (the mode
exists: `fixed_policy_evaluation_`, used by the abstract best response):
- **its own initialization path**: the current `initialize` allocates the regret table unconditionally
  (`trainer.cpp:597-601`) before the fixed-policy branch (`:618-621`), which would double part A's memory
  (4.2). The part-A path allocates only the policy (8 B per cell), the class cache and the chunk's boards: no
  regrets, no strategy sums, no discount timestamps;
- load the average `BucketPolicy` into the dense slots;
- for each board and each seat, run the 3-seat traversal of section 3 with no regret or sum writes;
- at the seat's preflop decision nodes, accumulate
  `cfv[node][class][action] += w_B · Σ_{h ∈ class, live} P(o1,o2|h)·v_a[h]` and the class weight
  `wt[class] += w_B · #(combos of the class live on B)` (P(h) cancels in the ratio);
- at the root, accumulate EV_s the same way.

**Board lists.**
- Exact: the 605,088 ordered histories with flop-orbit weights, the board set of the HU exact evaluator.
- Sampled: K physical flops, weight 1/K each (6.1).
- Checkdown tree (V11 only): the 19,998 canonical 5-card boards with orbit weights, in `board_kernels` mode.
- Boards are processed per flop chunk (1,056 boards). The state file keeps the running sums per flop chunk
  (54 × 81 × 4 doubles per seat: kilobytes), so a pass can stop at 19:40 and resume. This is far simpler than
  the certifier's state.

**Outputs:** a JSON compatible with `monker_in_our_game.py` and `decompose_br.py`, with per seat:
- per-combo `combo_values[node][action][h]` = cfv[node][class(h)][action] / wt[class(h)], the same value for
  every combo of a class (exact per combo by suit symmetry for the exact list; the class estimate for a
  sampled list), and per-combo `root_values` built the same way;
- own reach and EV;
- `opponent_reach[node][h]`, which **must be a probability** for the tool's `root_identity`
  (`monker_in_our_game.py:140-152`, which fits one constant to 1 − Σ_top `opponent_reach`): the probability
  that the other two seats reach the node given h, with card removal. Preflop reach is class-constant, so it is
  computed from the class cache: `opponent_reach[node][h] = D3_class(node, class(h)) / 278,256`, which equals
  the board-weighted Σ_B w_B·[h live]·P(o1,o2|h)·D3_B(h) / Σ_B w_B·[h live] (tower property, 3.9);
- expected rake;
- the folded-cards convention;
- the board list (exact, or K and the seed).

The fitted constant of `root_identity` is then the BTN's payoff when UTG and CO fold, and 0 for UTG and CO:
every UTG action leads to a CO decision, so for UTG and CO the ended mass 1 − Σ_top `opponent_reach` is 0.
The tool's per-combo inputs (`:64-90`) are otherwise unchanged; the 3-seat JSON gets `heroes` of length 3.

**Preflop-only best response per seat.** An exact recursion over the seat's own preflop nodes (the
counterfactual values do not depend on the seat's own preflop policy), as `monker_in_our_game.py` does.

**Why the trainer and not a new street-aggregated evaluator.**
- It reuses the validated 3-seat kernels, class cache and shortcut. Its C++ side (A1) costs 3.5-4.5 h against
  12-20 h for a new evaluator; part A in total is 6.5-9 h (8.2).
- The price: flop and turn node work is repeated for each of the 1,056 runouts per flop. The kernels dominate
  anyway (section 3.11). *Estimate:* 1.5-2 times slower than an aggregated evaluator, which is acceptable on
  the server.
- Part B needs the aggregated form anyway (the best response takes a max per information set over runouts),
  so it is built then.

**Validation of part A** (V11):
- On the HU G1 policy, with the N = 2 path, the preflop action values and EVs must equal
  `gtosd_preflop_blueprint_monker_values --all-flops` within 1e-9 a.
- On the 3WAY50 checkdown tree with a step-1 policy, **in `board_kernels` mode** over the 19,998 canonical
  5-card boards, EV and per-seat gains must equal `gtosd_preflop_blueprint_checkdown_classes --folded-cards
  dead` within 1e-9 a (a single evaluation, no trajectory, so the exact tolerance applies). The same check in
  `class_cache` mode is kept only as a plumbing test: it is circular (3.9).
- The sum of EVs must equal minus the expected rake, the latter computed independently at the terminals.
- `monker_in_our_game.py` accepts the JSON and its `root_identity` residual is below 1e-9 for every seat, with
  the fitted constant equal to the BTN's UTG-and-CO-fold payoff for the BTN and 0 for UTG and CO.

Known limit (diary 5.9): a postflop learned against fixed ranges has no reason to discourage preflop

### 6.4 MonkerSolver in our game, 3 seats

For each seat s, from the same part-A pass: the EV of MonkerSolver's rows for s against ours, with the other
seats and the whole postflop ours. The counterfactual values of s do not depend on s's preflop policy, so this

The game is not zero-sum with 3 seats, so the other seats' EV changes are reported too. That needs one extra
pass with s's rows replaced (`replace_preflop_rows`, `monker_chart_exploitation.hpp:81-162`); it is optional

## 7. Validation plan (the phase-3a gate, plus V11 for 3b)

**Tolerances** (revised; the first version's "1e-12 relative" fails on masses that come out of a subtraction):
- **Kernel masses (V2, V3):** |x − ref| ≤ 1e-12 · max(1, D_ref[h]), D = the hand's joint deal mass (D3 for the
  2-active masses). Not the repo's `close()` (|a−b| ≤ tol·max(1,|ref|),
  `tests/preflop_blueprint_test_support.hpp:50-52`): L and Lose can be −O(1e-11)·D when their exact value is 0
  (3.5; in critique 1's replica 15 of 17 such hands failed `close(·,·,1e-12)`).
- **Sums of signed terms (V8):** relative to Σ|terms|.
- **Traversal values and regret increments (V6, V7 one iteration, V9b, V13):** relative to the largest |v_a| of
  the row (or of the hand, for per-hand values).
- **Exact-value equalities from one evaluation (V11):** 1e-9 a.
- **Trajectory comparisons across different summation orders (V7 100 iterations, V9):** 1e-6 a on EVs and
  gains and 0.001 on chart frequencies (phase 2a critique, item 7); regret matching can amplify sign flips of
  near-zero regrets, so no bit or 1e-9 equality is asked of trajectories.

- **V1. HU byte identity.** Everything below must be byte-identical to a build of `061f167`:
  - `ctest -L "preflop_blueprint|card_abstraction"` 90/90;
  - HU50 G1 (15×4, TX2, donk) with `--chart-every 100 --policy-snapshots` for 200 iterations: trainer
    identity, checkpoint bytes, policy fingerprint and chart files;
  - the same for `HU50_step2_donk_rake.json` and one HU10 fixture.
- **V2. Kernel brute force.** O(n³) references (`*_reference`) for:
  - the 5 masses of the 3-active kernel;
  - the 3 masses of the 2-active kernel, with each seat in turn as folder;
  - D3.

  Cases:
  - 30 boards, including tie-heavy ones: the board plays (T-J-Q-K-A on board, full house on board, quads on
    board), paired and flush boards, the A-6-7-8-9 straight;
  - dense random reach, sparse reach (20 hands per seat) and reach with exact zeros;
  - every hero seat.

  D-scaled tolerance (above). A deliberately swapped seat pair must fail (the masses are compared one by one,
  so a lower/higher swap shows as Tℓ ↔ Tu even where the payoffs cannot tell them apart).
- **V3. Uniform folded seat.** With r_f ≡ 1, the 2-active masses equal 351 × the HU masses (`showdown_masses`
  worse and tied, `fold_mass`) on every board, with the D-scaled tolerance.
- **V4. Class cache.**
  - Terms exist only for `street == Preflop` nodes: on the step-2 3WAY50, exactly 36 all-in and 25 fold terms;
    on the checkdown-compiled 3WAY50, the same plus 15 checkdown leaves (3.9).
  - Tensors equal those of `three_way_terminals`: a byte comparison **per preflop node id on the
    checkdown-compiled 3WAY50**, the only tree where the step-1 builder runs (it throws on the first postflop
    showdown, `checkdown_classes.hpp:535-536`).
  - Scale check: for 3 hero combos, enumerate every board where h is live and compare the mean of the
    board-restricted brute-force value with cache × 1 within 1e-12 · max(1, D3) (D-scaled, as the kernel
    masses). The 0.512140 factor is checked there.
  - The sampled-board oracle of §3.9 agrees in mean within 4 standard errors over 10,000 boards (a smoke test;
    V9b is its exact form).
- **V5. Losing and folded payoffs.** The creation asserts pass on every 3WAY50 fixture. A doctored
  `CompiledGame` with an unequal loser payoff is refused.
- **V6. Shortcut.** On 3WAY50 (rake), with **`--partition-target 64`** (the runner's value) and 2 and 8
  threads, on 50 random boards and random policies: root values and regret increments with the shortcut on and
  off are equal within the row-scaled 1e-12. Before each hero pass the buffers of skipped units are filled
  with NaN (`TrainerConfig::poison_skipped_units`, a debug flag that cannot change a correct result and is not
  part of the identity). The test asserts that each hero has at least one skipped unit root and logs the
  count, so the stale-buffer case of 3.2 is actually exercised.
- **V7. "3-way with a seat that never plays" = HU** (virtual dead seat).
  - The literal test (a 3-way tree with UTG always folding against an HU tree) cannot be exact:
    - config validation requires `ante > 0` (`game_config.cpp:260`), so UTG's dead ante makes the pot 4a
      instead of 3a;
    - the pot-sized open is 6a against HU50's 5a.
  - Instead, a diagnostic `TrainerConfig::three_seat_harness`, allowed only on an HU game and part of the
    identity: the HU game runs through the 3-seat code path with a virtual third seat that never acts,
    reach ≡ 1, inactive everywhere with payoff 0, and pair weight 1/(406·351).
  - Preflop terminals use the HU pair table in this mode. The class cache is covered by V4, V9 and V9b.
  - Requirement, in two parts (the harness computes D3 as A·B − Σ + P with r_f ≡ 1 instead of `fold_mass`, so
    its increments differ from the HU path at rounding level):
    - **one iteration** (HU50 G1 and HU50_rake): regret increments and root values equal the HU path within
      the row-scaled 1e-12, where the equality is exact up to rounding;
    - **100 iterations**: 1e-6 a on EVs and 0.001 on charts (the trajectory rule).
  - This covers: three reach arrays, a frozen folded vector, 2-active kernels, D3 at folds, the weight, and
    unit handling. It does **not** cover: the hero on the BTN, a folder in the lower seat, a non-constant frozen
    vector, the 3-active kernel, the hero-folded shortcut (in HU a hero fold ends the hand), or a third vector
    passed through a non-hero decision; its virtual seat is always the higher seat with reach ≡ 1. V13 covers
    those.
- **V8. Rake and zero-sum identity at the vector level.** For each 3WAY50 rake fixture, on 20 boards and every
  postflop terminal, with random reach for all three seats:
  - Σ_s ⟨r_s, V_s⟩ = −rake(t)·Σ_triples r_0·r_1·r_2, the rake taken per winner set through the masses;
  - ⟨r_s, D3_s⟩ is equal for s = 0, 1, 2.

  Tolerance relative to Σ|terms|. It runs twice: through the kernel API, and **through `terminal3` with each
  seat as hero** (via the `TrainerAccess` hook of V13), so the trainer's seat, folder and payoff mapping is
  checked too, not only the kernels.
- **V9. Consistency with step 1 on the checkdown tree.**
  - Train CLI with `--checkdown` (compiles with `checkdown_at_flop`) and an exact weighted list of the 19,998
    suit-canonical 5-card boards (the count was computed at 13:28 and rechecked by critique 1; weights = orbit
    sizes, total 376,992).
  - **HU leg:** HU50 and HU50_rake (step-2 trainer against `checkdown_classes --reference`), which proves the
    DCFR schedules coincide. It is not circular: HU preflop terminals use board-restricted masses (pair table,
    `fold_mass`).
  - **3-way leg, in `board_kernels` mode (3.9).** In `class_cache` mode this leg would be circular: every
    checkdown leaf is a preflop terminal, so the cache (built by the class solver's own code) would supply every
    value and no kernel of 3.5-3.7 would run. In `board_kernels` mode every checkdown leaf, preflop all-in and
    preflop fold is evaluated on the listed board by the kernels and the board-restricted D3. With every board
    enumerated, the regret increments are exactly proportional per class row to the class solver's.
    - Fixtures: 3WAY50_donk_rake and 3WAY50_donk_rake25cap2, against `checkdown_classes --folded-cards dead`,

  V13 these errors would pass: splitting the wrong non-hero vector at a postflop decision in a 3-way pot;
  taking the folded seat from the wrong bit; misordering lower and higher only after a postflop fold; a wrong
  frozen vector for a seat that folded postflop. Value-level checks cannot separate lower from higher: with
  10,000 units per ante (`money.hpp:43`) and odd units to the lowest seats (`game.cpp:636-646`), p({h,ℓ}) and

    node after a postflop fold in a 3-way pot with each seat in turn as folder. Heroes 0, 1 and 2 at each root
    (where the hero is inactive, the expected value is the shortcut's payoff × D3).
  - Injected reach: about 20 random hands per seat with random positive weights; the folded seat's vector is
    **not** constant.
  - Hook: `TrainerAccess` (the friend class of `trainer.hpp:431`, defined at `trainer.cpp:2392`) gets

    production top phase and units (partition target 64, 2 threads) and records through a debug sink (null in
    production, one pointer test per hero node) the per-hand action values at every hero decision and the
    regret-table delta.
  - Oracle: an independent scalar recursive walker in the test file. It enumerates explicit triples (h, o1, o2)
    (h among 20 hero hands, o1 and o2 among the injected hands, mutually disjoint and disjoint from the board),
    walks the subtree with scalar probabilities from the same policy rows, ranks the 7-card hands with the
    equity evaluator (`include/gtosd/equity/evaluator.hpp`, `evaluate_seven_batch`), not with `BoardContext`'s
    rank table, and reads `showdown_payoffs` and `fold_payoffs` per winner set. About 8,000 triples per
    terminal: milliseconds per root.
  - Compared: v_a[h] at every hero decision and v[h] at the root for the 20 hero hands (tolerance relative to
    Σ|terms|); for one hero row per root, the regret delta against Σ_h weight·(v_a − v) from the sink values.

### 8.4 Risks

1. **The kernel is slower than the estimate.** The revised estimate (tie terms on about 99 % of hands) already
   uses the whole margin of the first version: computed 1.5-3.6 s per iteration, planned 2-4.5 s. At 2 times
   the computed cost a 15×4 run takes three or more i3 windows.
   - Mitigations: the fused loops of 3.5-3.6 (required, not optional); the G form for static sets; AVX2; D3
     caching; a cost-weighted partition. (Skipping tie terms for singleton groups is not a mitigation: it
     saves about 1 %.)
   - Last resort: MAP 2's rule "multiway may sample". Sample o2 given o1 on some terminals; unbiased, but it
     adds variance.
   - K4 settles this before T is finished.
2. **HU regression.** The separate N = 3 path and V1 guard against it. The risk is divergence between the two
   paths when later fixes land in only one: note it in the trainer header comment.
3. **Seat mapping errors** (lower/higher, the folder's transpose; the step-1 critique item 8 bug class).
   V2 covers every seat as hero and as folder at the kernel level; V8 runs through `terminal3` with each seat
   as hero; V9 (in `board_kernels` mode) and V9b check the preflop terminals against the independent class
   table; **V13** checks the postflop traversal (vector splits, folder bit, lower/higher after a postflop fold,
   frozen folded vectors) against a scalar walker. None of the earlier checks alone was exact for seat
   mapping: in `class_cache` mode V9 never runs a kernel, and values barely distinguish lower from higher
   (V13).
3b. **Stale unit values** (3.2): prevented by taking the shortcut before the unit lookup; exercised by V6 and
   V10 at partition target 64 with poisoned buffers.
4. **3-player CFR does not settle.** There is no Nash guarantee, so the chart change may plateau above the
   threshold (0.008, or 0.005 under the alternative).
   - The cap (48,000, or 64,000) bounds it.
   - Step 1 converged to gains of 1.7e-5 to 1.6e-4 % of the pot (diary 9.5): reassuring, but it had no
     sampling.
5. **Memory on the i3** with anything else running: 15×4 double needs about 14.8 GB. No HU run and no part A
   can share the machine (4.2).
6. **Partition imbalance** at 100+ threads: cost-weighted units, and a measurement in the first server run.
7. **Server logistics.** CMake presets are Windows-only (`CMakePresets.json`), so a Linux server (Hetzner)
   needs a port. Use a Windows host (phoenixNAP d1.c4.medium, 2 × Gold 6258R, 56 cores, about 0.68 €/h on
   28/09) or AWS Windows. NUMA with 2 sockets: first-touch placement of 13-27 GB; measure.
   - A fresh host needs Build Tools, CMake, the vcpkg bootstrap and dependencies and a first build: **1.5-3 h
     (*estimate*, unmeasured)** before the first smoke run, counted in the rental.
   - At least 400 GB of disk (4.3). Upload is small (`out/preflop_blueprint_resources` 407 MB, a bucket set
     42 MB; critique 2).
   - Keep/download list before the rental ends: chart snapshots, part-A JSON, `run.log`/`train.jsonl`, and the
     final policies only if part B will need them (13.56 GB each at 30×4). Checkpoints are discarded.
8. **Disk.** 27-34 GB at rest and 41-48 GB peak per 15×4 run with `POLICY_SNAPSHOT_EVERY=16000` (4.3). C: has
   272 GB free and F: 251 GB (14:02); archiving runs at 31-39 MB/s and only between 20:00 and 24:00.
8b. **Saves near the 20:00 limit** (4.3, 5.4): 1.3-4.5 min per checkpoint-only daily stop, slowed about 4.5
   times by a concurrent archive. Hence the 19:40 STOP and no archive during a save.
9. **Rake evidence conflicts** (D2). The run is repeated under a second rake on the server.
10. **Modelling.** The per-board buckets are heads-up equity features (against one random hand), reused for 3
    seats. MonkerSolver's settings screens show the same generic counts for any player count. A strength
    against two random hands is a later experiment.

## 11. Critique (self-review at writing time, updated at the revision)

1. **Every timing is a model.**
   - The kernel figure (now 375-520 Mflop per board and hero pass) assumes 650-870 flops per hand for 3-active
     terminals with ties on about 99 % of hands (measured share of tied hands; the flops are not measured).
   - The throughput (20-50 GFLOP/s aggregate, 1.2-3 flops per cycle per physical core) is a guess for this loop
     on the i3.
   - The traversal figure is scaled from HU G4, which has HU kernels and two reach vectors.
   - If the true cost is 2 times the computed one (3-7.2 s per iteration), the i3 does 10,000-23,000
     iterations per window and a 15×4 run takes two to four windows.
   - K4 must come before the timeline is trusted.
2. **The class cache is a departure from HU practice.** HU restricts opponents to the board at preflop
   terminals; the cache is board-free. Both are unbiased (§3.9); V9 in `board_kernels` mode and V9b prove the
   equality exactly (the first version's claim that V9 proves it in cache mode was circular). But the variance
   trade-off on the *mixed* preflop nodes (actions that lead to the postflop are board-sampled, all-in and fold
   values are not) was argued, not measured.
3. **"3-way needs about the HU iteration count"** rests on per-row board samples, which the lane-B replication
   verified exactly for HU. It ignores two things:
   - the policy-refresh granularity (preflop rows get only T regret-matching steps per seat);
   - 3-player dynamics, which have no convergence guarantee.

   The 24,000-40,000 range is an analogy, not a prediction, and it holds only with the HU-equivalent stop
   threshold (0.008 on the non-all-in charts; 5.1).
4. **V7 is not the literal "one seat always folds" test.** Config validation forces a dead ante, so no HU game
   matches. The harness gives an exact equality at one iteration only, adds test-only code to the trainer (part
   of its identity), and never exercises a real third seat (V13 does).
5. **Part A through the trainer** repeats flop and turn work for every runout. On the i3 the exact pass (8-18 h)
   is marginal, and the section 10 timeline puts it on the server. Without a server the final evaluation of the
   first run is sampled only (physical flops, with standard errors).
6. **The 18-chart stop metric** averages fewer charts, so it is noisier; some of the 18 are low-reach nodes.
   The 3-way seed noise is unknown until the server's seed-2 run.
7. **D2 rests on conflicting evidence** (HU chart match versus 3-way EV indifference and the step-1 lock). The
   recommendation depends on test 7, whose result was not available.
8. **The timeline assumes three agents in parallel** and the speed of phases 2a and 2b. Phase 3a touches the
   hottest validated code (the trainer), where review cycles have been longer. The revised calibrated finish
   (21:00-02:00) makes D6 the difference between a run starting at 00:00 and one starting at 01:30-03:00.
9. **Anchors and census.**
   - Terminal and cell counts come from the phase-1 Python oracle and MAP 2, which match the C++ totals.
   - The per-hero "hero-active" counts in §3.11 use the symmetric two-thirds approximation, not an exact count
     per seat.
   - `run_step2_continuous.sh` anchors are from MAP 3; this spec did not read the script.
10. **Code duplication.** The separate N = 3 traversal protects HU bytes but doubles the code a future change
    must touch. Merge the two paths only after 3-way results exist and V1 can be rerun.
11. **The "ignore" convention has no step-2 form.** Under board-first sampling, dead is the natural model; the
    60a probe supports it, and `--folded-cards ignore` is rejected. The step-1 dead-versus-ignore comparison
    therefore cannot be repeated in step 2.
12. **3-way reference numbers not computed here:** the step-1 suited preference, and range difference by
    chart group. They were left out to respect the no-`out/**` rule; the viewer or `compare_charts` can add
    them.
